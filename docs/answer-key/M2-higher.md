### Question 1 — M2H-01

**Skill:** CAS.WIC Words in Context · **Difficulty:** Easy, rating 2/5 · **Target time:** 45s · **Topic:** Falsifiability in scientific theories

A theory earns scientific status, philosopher Elena Marsh argues, not because it can be confirmed but because it can be \_\_\_\_\_\_: some observation could, in principle, show it to be false. A theory compatible with every conceivable outcome, she contends, tells us nothing about the world, however satisfying it seems.

**Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?**

A) celebrated  
B) simplified  
C) popularized  
D) refuted

**Key: D.** The passage defines scientific status by the possibility that an observation could "show it to be false," so the blank must mean capable of being shown false, which is what "refuted" means.

- **A — Wrong connotation:** "Celebrated" carries a tone of praise, not the neutral, testable quality of being provably false that the passage describes.
- **B — Illogical in context:** Whether a theory is simple has no bearing on whether an observation could show it false, so "simplified" does not fit the passage's logic.
- **C — Out of scope:** The passage never discusses a theory's popularity; it discusses only whether observation can disprove it.

### Question 2 — M2H-02

**Skill:** CAS.WIC Words in Context · **Difficulty:** Medium, rating 3/5 · **Target time:** 60s · **Topic:** Fiction: a violinist and her rival

When Ana first heard Mireille play the same concerto she herself had spent a decade perfecting, she expected envy. Instead, sitting in the shadowed balcony, she felt something closer to \_\_\_\_\_\_: a reluctant, uncomfortable respect for a rival whose phrasing revealed possibilities she had never considered in the piece.

**Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?**

A) resentment  
B) indifference  
C) admiration  
D) nostalgia

**Key: C.** The passage explains the feeling as "a reluctant, uncomfortable respect" for a rival's skill, which is precisely what "admiration" names.

- **A — Wrong connotation:** Resentment is too negative for a feeling the text describes as respect, and the text explicitly contrasts this feeling with the envy Ana expected.
- **B — Contradicts the text:** The text says Ana felt "something closer to" a strong reaction, not the absence of feeling that "indifference" implies.
- **D — Out of scope:** Nothing in the passage refers to Ana recalling or missing the past; the reaction is about the present performance.

### Question 3 — M2H-03

**Skill:** CAS.WIC Words in Context · **Difficulty:** Hard, rating 4/5 · **Target time:** 75s · **Topic:** Sleep and memory consolidation

Rather than passively fading, memories encoded during the day are, according to recent hypotheses, actively \_\_\_\_\_\_ during subsequent slow-wave sleep: the hippocampus appears to replay recent neural activity, nudging fragile traces toward more durable, cortex-based storage rather than merely allowing them to decay unaltered.

**Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?**

A) suppressed  
B) randomized  
C) erased  
D) reorganized

**Key: D.** The passage describes memory traces being "nudged toward more durable, cortex-based storage" through replay, which is a process of reorganizing where and how a memory is stored, not eliminating or scrambling it.

- **B — Illogical in context:** The hippocampus is described as replaying recent activity in a directed way toward durable storage, not scrambling it without pattern, so "randomized" fits neither the mechanism nor the outcome described.
- **C — Contradicts the text:** The passage states traces move toward "more durable" storage rather than decaying, which is the opposite of being erased.

### Question 4 — M2H-04

**Skill:** CAS.TSP Text Structure and Purpose · **Difficulty:** Medium, rating 3/5 · **Target time:** 60s · **Topic:** Original poem: migrating cranes

*From Marguerite Solano's poem "Passage of Cranes" (fictional)*

**Passage of Cranes**

> They lift before the frost has named the field,  
> wedge after wedge stitched loose against the gray,  
> calling down a distance no map has sealed,  
> old thread pulled taut from one blue year to the next.  
> <u>Below, the harvest goes on without them, unhurried, as if their leaving were only weather.</u>  
> Yet something in the barns leans toward the sky,  
> some small attention tilted north and south,  
> tracking what the cranes already know.

**Which choice best describes the function of the underlined sentence in the text as a whole?**

A) It highlights how ordinary the cranes' departure appears to those on the ground, setting up the contrast with the quiet attentiveness described afterward.  
B) It suggests that farmers are generally indifferent to the changing of the seasons each year.

**Key: A.** The underlined sentence shows life on the ground continuing "unhurried," as if the cranes' leaving were unremarkable, which sets up the following lines about a quieter, less obvious attentiveness in the barns.

- **B — Too broad:** The sentence is specifically about how the cranes' departure looks from below, not a general claim about farmers' attitude toward the seasons.
- **C — Out of scope:** The poem never suggests the harvest failed or that the cranes' timing affected it; the sentence only describes the harvest continuing.
- **D — Wrong function:** The sentence does not criticize anyone; it establishes a contrast in attention, which the rest of the poem develops rather than a turn toward criticism.

### Question 5 — M2H-05

**Skill:** CAS.TSP Text Structure and Purpose · **Difficulty:** Hard, rating 4/5 · **Target time:** 75s · **Topic:** The race to build clocks accurate enough for navigation at sea

For centuries, sailors could fix their latitude by the sun's height but not their longitude, since that required knowing the time at a home port precisely enough to compare against local noon. Pendulum clocks, reliable on land, faltered aboard a rolling ship, and proposed alternatives, such as lunar tables demanding laborious calculation or magnetic variation charts of dubious consistency, solved the problem only partially, trading one uncertainty for another. Not until instrument makers addressed the clock itself, rather than devising workarounds for its failure, did dependable navigation at sea become routine.

**Which choice best states the main purpose of the text?**

A) To explain why solving the problem of a ship's longitude required a more fundamental fix than earlier partial workarounds provided  
B) To argue that lunar tables were more reliable than magnetic variation charts for determining longitude  
C) To describe the process by which pendulum clocks were adapted for use on land  
D) To claim that sailors before the era of accurate clocks were unable to determine their position at sea

**Key: A.** The text traces failed workarounds and concludes that only a fix to "the clock itself" solved the longitude problem, which is the point the whole passage builds toward.

- **B — Too narrow:** The text mentions both alternatives only to note that each solved the problem partially; it never ranks the two workarounds against each other.
- **C — Contradicts the text:** Pendulum clocks are described as already reliable on land and faltering at sea, not as being adapted for land use.
- **D — Too extreme:** The text says sailors could determine latitude, so it does not support the extreme claim that they could not determine their position at all.

### Question 6 — M2H-06

**Skill:** CAS.CTC Cross-Text Connections · **Difficulty:** Medium, rating 3/5 · **Target time:** 70s · **Topic:** Fictional pilot programmes giving residents regular cash payments: two economists

**Text 1**

In a fictional pilot program in the town of Verrenne, Dr. Ilse Kovar found that households receiving a modest, no-strings monthly payment increased spending at local shops within months, and several new small businesses opened nearby. Kovar concludes that predictable cash support can act as a reliable stimulus for local economies.

**Text 2**

Examining the same Verrenne data eighteen months after payments ended, Dr. Tomas Whitfield found that shop revenues and new-business survival rates had mostly returned to pre-program levels. Whitfield cautions that short-term upticks observed while payments are ongoing may not indicate a lasting change in the local economy.

**Based on the texts, how would Whitfield most likely respond to Kovar's claim that predictable cash support is a reliable stimulus for local economies?**

B) By arguing that the payments caused shop revenues to fall below their original levels

**Key: D.** Whitfield found that revenues and business survival returned to earlier levels after payments ended, which supports the idea, stated in his own words, that upticks during payments may not last.

- **B — Reverses the relationship:** Whitfield found revenues returned to their earlier levels, not that they fell below them.

### Question 7 — M2H-07

**Skill:** CAS.CTC Cross-Text Connections · **Difficulty:** Hard, rating 5/5 · **Target time:** 100s · **Topic:** Why a fictional ancient city was abandoned: two archaeologists

**Text 1**

Archaeologist Nadia Petrova has argued that the city of Kessa was abandoned because of a prolonged drought. Sediment cores from a nearby lakebed show decades of markedly reduced rainfall coinciding closely with the layers marking the city's decline, and Petrova considers this climatic evidence sufficient to explain the exodus.

**Text 2**

Archaeologist Farrukh Aliyev points instead to trade-route records showing that Kessa's principal trading partners had shifted to alternate routes decades before the driest years recorded in the lakebed cores. Since population decline in the city's layers appears to begin before the drought's onset, Aliyev argues that economic isolation better explains the abandonment.

**Which choice best describes a difference in how the authors of Text 1 and Text 2 view the cause of Kessa's abandonment?**

A) Petrova attributes the abandonment to declining trade, while Aliyev attributes it to drought.  
B) Petrova and Aliyev agree that drought and declining trade contributed equally to the abandonment.  
C) Petrova points to climatic evidence that coincides with the decline, while Aliyev points to economic evidence that precedes it.  
D) Petrova argues the abandonment happened gradually, while Aliyev argues it happened abruptly within a single season.

**Key: C.** Petrova's evidence, the sediment cores, coincides in time with the city's decline, while Aliyev's evidence, the trade-route shift, predates the decline; this timing distinction is exactly what separates their explanations.

- **A — Wrong text attribution:** This swaps the two researchers' positions: Petrova points to drought and Aliyev points to trade, not the reverse.
- **B — Contradicts the text:** The two researchers propose competing, not complementary, explanations, and neither text describes the causes as contributing equally.
- **D — Out of scope:** Neither text addresses the abandonment's pace or speed; both texts instead discuss the timing of causes, not how quickly the abandonment itself unfolded.

### Question 9 — M2H-09

### Question 10 — M2H-10

**Skill:** INI.COET Command of Evidence: Textual · **Difficulty:** Hard, rating 4/5 · **Target time:** 75s · **Topic:** A fictional study of gift exchange in a fishing community

Anthropologist Grace Odhiambo has argued that in the fishing village of Talenge, gift-giving of surplus catch functions less as generosity than as a form of informal insurance: households that give freely during abundant seasons are, she claims, the same households most likely to receive support when their own catches later fail.

**Which finding, if true, would most directly support Odhiambo's claim?**

**Key: A.** Odhiambo's claim is that generous givers are the ones who later receive support, so a record showing exactly that correlation directly supports her insurance-like reciprocity claim.

- **C — Out of scope:** A shift toward market selling does not address whether past generosity predicts later support, which is the specific claim in question.
- **D — True but irrelevant:** A general decline in yields says nothing about whether generous givers are the ones who receive support later.

### Question 14 — M2H-14

**Skill:** INI.INF Inferences · **Difficulty:** Hard, rating 5/5 · **Target time:** 90s · **Topic:** Dark matter in small galaxies: a fictional survey

A survey of dwarf galaxies orbiting a nearby spiral, conducted by astronomer Priya Sundaram's team, found that the smallest, faintest satellites showed rotation speeds implying far more mass than their visible stars could account for, consistent with dark matter dominating their gravity. Yet a handful of dwarfs of similar size and brightness showed rotation speeds implying comparatively little unseen mass, a pattern the team could not attribute to measurement error alone. If a single, uniform ratio of dark to visible matter governed every dwarf galaxy regardless of its history, Sundaram noted, such variation among otherwise similar satellites would be \_\_\_\_\_\_.

**Which choice most logically completes the text?**

**Key: D.** A uniform dark-to-visible matter ratio would predict that similarly sized, similarly bright dwarfs contain similar proportions of unseen mass, so the observed variation among such similar satellites would be hard to reconcile with that hypothesis.

- **A — Illogical in context:** A uniform ratio tied to brightness would predict consistency among similarly bright dwarfs, not the variation the team actually observed, so this does not follow from the passage's premise.
- **B — Contradicts the text:** The passage describes a handful of dwarfs departing from the otherwise dominant pattern, not most dwarfs showing identical rotation speeds.
- **C — Illogical in context:** The passage's entire argument rests on rotation speed reflecting the distribution of mass, so claiming the two are unrelated contradicts the premise of the surrounding discussion.

### Question 15 — M2H-15

**Skill:** SEC.BND Boundaries · **Difficulty:** Easy, rating 2/5 · **Target time:** 45s · **Topic:** Deep-sea hydrothermal vents

Hydrothermal vents form where seawater seeps into cracks in the ocean floor and is heated by magma far below the seafloor\_\_\_\_\_\_the water that returns to the surface can exceed three hundred degrees Celsius without boiling because of the immense pressure at that depth.

**Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?**

A) , the water  
B) ; the water  
C) the water  
D) : and the water

**Key: B.** Both "Hydrothermal vents form...below the seafloor" and "the water that returns...that depth" are independent clauses, so a semicolon alone correctly joins them.

- **A — Comma splice:** A comma alone cannot join two independent clauses; this creates a comma splice.
- **C — Run-on (fused sentence):** With no punctuation at all, the two independent clauses are simply fused together, creating a run-on sentence.
- **D — Colon or semicolon misuse:** Following a colon with the coordinating conjunction "and" is nonstandard; a colon is not paired with a conjunction this way.

### Question 16 — M2H-16

A) ,  
B) ;  
C) :

**Key: A.** The phrase "a technique that records light arriving over seconds or even hours rather than a fraction of a second" is a nonessential appositive opened with a comma after "photography"; it must be closed with a matching comma before the main verb "transforms."

- **B — Unbalanced nonessential element:** The nonessential element was opened with a comma, so it must be closed with a comma; closing it with a semicolon leaves the punctuation marks mismatched.
- **C — Colon or semicolon misuse:** A colon is not the appropriate mark for closing a nonessential appositive that was opened with a comma.
- **D — Sentence fragment:** A period here splits the sentence into two fragments: the first has a subject and appositive but no main verb, and the second, "Transforms moving water...," has a verb but no subject.
