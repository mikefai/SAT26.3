### Question 1 — M2L-01

**Skill:** CAS.WIC Words in Context · **Difficulty:** Easy, rating 1/5 · **Target time:** 35s · **Topic:** Penguins huddling for warmth

Emperor penguins breed on Antarctic ice during the coldest months of the year. To survive winds that can strip away body heat, thousands of birds pack themselves into a \_\_\_\_\_\_ mass, each individual sheltering against its neighbors. Birds on the group's cold outer edge slowly shuffle toward the center, while those that had been sheltered take a turn on the perimeter, so that the huddle rotates and no single penguin is left exposed for long.

**Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?**

A) scattered  
B) fragile  
C) compact  
D) transparent

**Key: C.** The surrounding text describes birds packing tightly together and rotating so no one stays on the exposed edge, which matches 'compact,' a mass with little space between individuals.

- **A — Contradicts the text:** Scattered means spread apart, the opposite of penguins pressing together into one mass as the text describes.
- **B — Wrong connotation:** Fragile suggests something easily broken or weak, not the dense, sturdy grouping the text describes as protection from wind.
- **D — Out of scope:** Transparent concerns seeing through something, an idea about clarity or visibility that the passage never raises.

### Question 5 — M2L-05

**Skill:** CAS.TSP Text Structure and Purpose · **Difficulty:** Medium, rating 3/5 · **Target time:** 60s · **Topic:** Salt flats and why they are so level

Salt flats that form in enclosed desert basins can stretch for many kilometers with a surface so flat that elevation varies by only a few centimeters from edge to edge. Seasonal floods spread a thin sheet of water evenly across the basin; as that water evaporates, it deposits a fresh layer of dissolved minerals across the entire surface rather than piling minerals into mounds. <u>Over centuries, repeated flooding and evaporation smooth out any low spots and gradually wear down any high spots as well.</u> The result is one of the flattest natural surfaces on Earth.

**Which choice best describes the function of the underlined sentence in the text as a whole?**

A) It explains the repeated natural process that produces the extreme flatness described earlier in the text.  
B) It offers evidence that salt flats are flatter than any other surface on Earth.  
C) It restates the process of mineral deposition described in the previous sentence without adding new information.  
D) It shifts the focus from how salt flats form to how travelers use them.

**Key: A.** The sentence describes the cycle of flooding and evaporation that levels high and low spots, directly explaining the mechanism behind the extreme flatness the passage opens and closes with.

- **B — Too extreme:** The underlined sentence explains the leveling process itself; the passage's closing claim is only that salt flats are "one of the flattest" surfaces, so "flatter than any other surface on Earth" overstates what either sentence supports.
- **C — Wrong function:** The underlined sentence describes a different process, gradual leveling through repeated flooding and evaporation, not mineral deposition, and it adds the new point that this repetition wears down high spots as well as filling low ones.
- **D — Out of scope:** The passage never discusses how people use salt flats; it stays focused on their formation.

### Question 6 — M2L-06

**Skill:** CAS.CTC Cross-Text Connections · **Difficulty:** Medium, rating 3/5 · **Target time:** 70s · **Topic:** Homework and learning: two fictional education researchers

**Text 1**

Education researcher Petra Lindqvist argues that short, regular homework assignments help students consolidate what they learned in class that same day. In her studies of secondary school students, those who completed brief nightly review exercises scored higher on unit tests than students who received identical classroom instruction but did no additional practice at home.

**Text 2**

Education researcher Amara Osei contends that homework's apparent benefits often reflect which students already study effectively rather than what homework itself teaches. In her surveys, students who already reviewed their notes independently kept doing so whether or not homework was assigned, while students who did not already study rarely completed homework consistently enough for it to change their results.

**Which choice best describes a difference in how Lindqvist and Osei interpret evidence that homework correlates with higher test scores?**

A) Both researchers agree that homework causes the improvement, but they disagree about how much homework is optimal.  
B) Lindqvist attributes the correlation to students' independent study habits, while Osei attributes it to homework itself.  
C) Osei argues that homework should be replaced entirely by extra classroom instruction, a position Lindqvist rejects.  
D) Lindqvist treats the correlation as evidence that homework causes the improvement, while Osei attributes it mainly to differences in students' independent study habits.

**Key: D.** Lindqvist's studies compare identical instruction with and without homework and credit homework for the score gain, while Osei's surveys trace the same correlation to study habits that exist with or without homework being assigned.

- **A — Contradicts the text:** Osei explicitly doubts that homework itself causes the improvement, so the two researchers do not agree on causation.
- **B — Wrong text attribution:** This reverses the two positions: it is Osei, not Lindqvist, who attributes the correlation to study habits.
- **C — Out of scope:** Osei questions whether homework causes score gains but never proposes eliminating homework or replacing it with more classroom instruction.

### Question 7 — M2L-07

**Skill:** CAS.CTC Cross-Text Connections · **Difficulty:** Hard, rating 4/5 · **Target time:** 85s · **Topic:** Why zebras have stripes: two hypotheses

**Text 1**

Biologist Dana Kowalczyk argues that a zebra's stripes evolved primarily to reduce bites from blood-feeding flies. She points to experiments in which horses fitted with striped coverings were landed on far less often by flies than horses in solid-colored coverings, and she suggests the contrast between light and dark bands disrupts the polarized light patterns flies use to find a landing surface.

**Text 2**

Biologist Hana Iwata maintains that stripes primarily help zebras avoid detection by predators moving through tall, sunlit grass, where alternating light and dark bands break up the animal's outline. She notes that fly-landing experiments have also found reduced landings on animals wearing several other high-contrast patterns, not stripes alone, which she says weakens any claim that stripe pattern specifically explains the effect.

**Which choice best describes a difference in how Kowalczyk and Iwata would most likely interpret a new finding that fly landings decrease on any animal covering with strong light-dark contrast, regardless of its pattern?**

A) Kowalczyk would reject the finding outright, while Iwata would use it to argue that predator avoidance, not flies, drives stripe evolution.  
B) Both researchers would treat the finding as unrelated to their hypotheses, since it concerns contrast rather than stripes specifically.  
C) Iwata would see the finding as undermining her own hypothesis about predator avoidance.  
D) Kowalczyk would likely view the finding as consistent with her proposed mechanism, since she credits light-dark contrast, while Iwata would treat it as further evidence that stripes are not uniquely effective against flies.

**Key: D.** Kowalczyk attributes reduced landings to light-dark contrast disrupting polarized light, so a contrast-general effect fits her mechanism; Iwata already cites such findings to argue that stripes are not specifically responsible.

- **A — Too extreme:** Nothing suggests Kowalczyk would reject the finding outright rather than see it as consistent with her mechanism, and the passage does not say Iwata would use it to argue for predator avoidance specifically.
- **B — Contradicts the text:** The finding is directly related to both hypotheses, especially Iwata's point that contrast-based effects extend beyond stripes.
- **C — Reverses the relationship:** Iwata's hypothesis concerns predator avoidance, not fly landings, so a finding about fly landings and contrast would not undermine her own hypothesis; it undermines the rival fly hypothesis instead.

### Question 9 — M2L-09

### Question 14 — M2L-14

### Question 15 — M2L-15

**Skill:** SEC.BND Boundaries · **Difficulty:** Easy, rating 1/5 · **Target time:** 35s · **Topic:** Parrotfish producing sand on coral reefs

Parrotfish spend much of each day scraping algae from coral with their beak-like teeth \_\_\_\_\_\_ swallow bits of coral rock along with the algae and grind the rock into fine sand inside their guts.

**Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?**

A) they  
B) , and they  
C) , they  
D) ; and they

**Key: B.** The sentence contains two independent clauses; a comma followed by the coordinating conjunction 'and' correctly joins them, giving ', and they.'

- **A — Run-on (fused sentence):** Without any punctuation or conjunction, this choice fuses two independent clauses together with no separation.
- **C — Comma splice:** A comma alone cannot join two independent clauses; this choice lacks the coordinating conjunction needed alongside the comma.
- **D — Colon or semicolon misuse:** A semicolon should join independent clauses on its own, not be paired with a coordinating conjunction like 'and.'

### Question 16 — M2L-16

**Skill:** SEC.BND Boundaries · **Difficulty:** Easy, rating 1/5 · **Target time:** 35s · **Topic:** Handmade paper from plant fibres

Papermakers soak plant fibers such as mulberry bark or cotton rag until they break down into a soft pulp \_\_\_\_\_\_ pour the pulp through a mesh screen to drain the water and leave behind a thin, even sheet.

**Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?**

A) , and  
B) ; and  
C) and  
D) , they

**Key: C.** The subject 'papermakers' governs both 'soak' and 'pour' in a compound predicate, so no comma is needed before the coordinating conjunction 'and.'

- **A — Unneeded punctuation:** A comma is not needed here because 'and' joins two verbs sharing the same subject, not two independent clauses.
- **B — Colon or semicolon misuse:** A semicolon requires an independent clause on each side, but 'pour the pulp...' shares its subject with the first clause and is not independent.
- **D — Comma splice:** Adding 'they' creates a second independent clause joined to the first by only a comma, which is a comma splice.
